# Behavioral Marketing

## Purpose
Use behavioral science to improve attention, comprehension, trust, choice, and follow-through without relying on deceptive pressure.

## Invoke when
Use for offer framing, CTA design, objection handling, pricing presentation, social proof, defaults, choice architecture, and diagnosing why people hesitate.

## Principles
- Reduce cognitive load before adding persuasion.
- Make the desired action easy to understand and easy to complete.
- Use social proof that is specific and relevant to the audience.
- Use loss aversion, scarcity, and urgency only when factually true.
- Prefer concrete anchors and comparisons that help the customer evaluate value.
- Reduce uncertainty with guarantees, process clarity, proof, and expectations.
- Limit competing choices when they create paralysis.
- Match the behavioral intervention to the actual friction instead of applying generic “psychology hacks.”

## Tool behavior
When behavior can be observed through analytics, form completion, page interaction, campaign metrics, or customer feedback, inspect that evidence before diagnosing motivation.

## Collaboration
Use landing-page-cro for page architecture, marketing-copywriting for wording, experimentation for tests, and marketing-analytics for observed behavior.

## Output
Name the friction, the behavioral mechanism likely involved, the smallest credible intervention, and how to test whether it worked.
