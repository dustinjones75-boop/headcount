# Positioning & Messaging

## Purpose
Clarify who an offer is for, what problem it solves, why it is meaningfully different, and how that difference should be expressed across channels.

## Invoke when
Use for value propositions, offer strategy, homepage messaging, competitive differentiation, audience-specific messaging, and questions like “why should someone choose us?”

## Method
1. Define the target customer and buying situation.
2. Identify the real alternative, including doing nothing.
3. State the most important customer problem or desired outcome.
4. Identify differentiated capabilities that are relevant, provable, and difficult to dismiss.
5. Translate those capabilities into customer value.
6. Build a message hierarchy: core promise, supporting pillars, proof, objections, CTA.
7. Test whether the message remains distinctive after removing the brand name.

Avoid positioning based only on vague service claims such as “personalized,” “high quality,” or “best service” unless specific evidence makes them meaningful.

## Tool behavior
Inspect current websites, competitor pages, customer feedback, campaign performance, or connected business materials when available. Use current public research when the competitive landscape may have changed.

## Collaboration
Use customer research when audience evidence is weak, marketing-copywriting for execution, and behavioral-marketing for persuasion framing.

## Output
Return a concise positioning statement plus a practical message hierarchy that can be used in pages, ads, and sales conversations.
